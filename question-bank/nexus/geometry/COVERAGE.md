## 8. Gaps and limitations

- **Standards text.** The standards and their wording (`src/standards.py`) were written from the Common Core State Standards for High School Geometry as published. The official site (thecorestandards.org) could not be opened from the authoring environment because of its network policy, so a reviewer should spot-check the wording against the official page.
- **Subskills.** Each standard is split by its subparts, its listed objects (for example the five definitions in CO.A.1), and its verbs (prove, construct, derive, explain). The split is this bank's reading of the standard; a reviewer may split differently. Every subskill has a set whose five MAIN questions test it, and subskills whose verb is prove, derive, explain, construct, or give an argument have MAIN questions that require that product (checked by `src/validate.py`).
- **(+) standards.** The six (+) standards (SRT.D.9-11, C.A.4, GPE.A.3, GMD.A.2) form the ADVANCED EXTENSION strand (sets 88-96, `strand` = advanced extension (+)). They never appear as prerequisites of core sets.
- **Forward standards in later courses** are labeled with the course this bank places them in (`engine/hs_courses.py`: Algebra II, Probability & Statistics, Precalculus). Those collections do not exist yet; forward branches carry the standard code only.
- **Forward branches that apply an earlier-taught standard.** Fifteen forward branches use a Geometry standard that is the MAIN of an earlier set (for example a construction justified with a congruence proof, or a (+) result applied in a modeling standard). They are labeled cross-standard extension and state the new demand; VALIDATION.md lists them.
- **Backward branch counts.** Sets have 2-4 backward branches (most have 3). The prompt allows 2-7; more branches were not added where no further distinct prerequisite obstacle exists.
- **Proofs, constructions, and drawings** (types `work` and `plot`) need teacher grading or a drawing surface. Their grading notes give essential criteria, valid alternatives, and invalid arguments (proofs), or the construction marks to look for (constructions). The game's answer checker cannot grade these automatically.
- **Diagrams.** Figures show only given information. Figures whose labeled measures are not to scale say "Not drawn to scale". Three-dimensional solids are oblique sketches with hidden edges dashed, always marked not to scale.
- **Answer checking.** Questions marked "Any equivalent form" need a checker that accepts equivalent expressions (for example 2√5 and √20) or a person; "Form required" questions accept only the stated form (exact, rounded, equation form).
- **Verification limits.** `src/resolve.py` recomputes 239 numeric and exact answers with separate code; the other answers (proofs, explanations, classifications, constructions) were checked by reading, not by program. Thirteen review stems recur in different sets' backward branches; no stem repeats within a set.
- **No difficulty or hint fields**, as in the other collections: difficulty is carried by the section, and the grading note can be shown as feedback after grading.
